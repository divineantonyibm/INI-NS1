Section 01

What is IBM Network Intelligence?

IBM® Network Intelligence (INM) is an AI-native, software-as-a-service (SaaS) platform deployed and managed on IBM Cloud. It transforms complex, multi-vendor and multi-domain network data into actionable intelligence — automatically detecting anomalies, generating root-cause hypotheses, and orchestrating remediation through agentic AI agents.

Built on IBM's advanced watsonx AI platform, Network Intelligence uses domain-trained time-series foundation models (Quantile, Prophet) and Network Operations Center (NOC) AI agents to process streaming and historical telemetry data from IP and Radio Access Network (RAN) domains. A single distributed node can process over one million indicators every five minutes, with capacity horizontally scalable by adding nodes.

ℹ

Plain language definition: Think of your network as a city with thousands of roads, intersections, and vehicles. Traditional monitoring tools are like traffic cameras — they capture what's happening but can't tell you why there's a jam or what to do about it. IBM Network Intelligence is a smart traffic control AI that not only sees every issue across every road but automatically figures out the root cause, writes a repair plan, and can dispatch a fix — with or without a human in the loop.

How Network Intelligence is structured

Network Intelligence is built on two core architectural pillars — Analytics and Automation — supported by four foundational components that work together to deliver end-to-end intelligence.

01

Scalable streaming ingestion of real-time and historical time-series data. Supports metrics, events, and logs regardless of data cleanliness.

02

AI Pipeline

Domain-trained time-series foundation models for ultra-low latency inferencing, early warning detection, and outlier identification from telemetry data.

03

Interprets data across domains and vendors with semantic understanding of network topology, grounding AI models in network-specific knowledge.

04

Agentic Framework

NOC AI agents with reasoning, RAG, and function-calling capabilities. Generates remediation plans executable autonomously or with human oversight.

Pain Points Addressed

The problems Network Intelligence was built to solve

Critical networks face persistent challenges: increasing complexity across multiple domains and vendors, high volumes of noisy alerts, manual lifecycle operations, and overlapping generations of technologies — all of which lead to slow incident response and costly downtime.

Before IBM Network Intelligence With IBM Network Intelligence
NOC operators are overwhelmed by thousands of alerts per day, most of them false positives or low-priority noise. Engineers spend hours triaging before even beginning to investigate. AI semantic understanding filters noise, surfacing only actionable high-confidence anomalies. Operators see a prioritized list of genuine issues — not a flood of alerts.
When a degradation occurs, engineers must manually correlate data across multiple monitoring tools, vendor dashboards, and logs — a process that can take hours and requires deep domain expertise. NOC AI agents automatically cross-correlate anomalies across devices, objects, and indicators to generate root-cause hypotheses with semantic context — in minutes, not hours.
Remediation steps live in siloed runbooks, Methods of Procedure (MOP) documents, and tribal knowledge. New engineers struggle to act quickly, and repeat incidents are common. Grounding documents (MOPs, config guides, knowledge base) are uploaded once and used by RAG agents to generate specific, contextual remediation plans for every anomaly.
Multi-vendor, multi-domain environments require integrating data from dozens of different sources, each with incompatible formats and APIs. Building a unified view is a bespoke, expensive project. Network Intelligence's data pipeline ingests data from any source — streaming or historical — regardless of format or data cleanliness, normalising it into a unified metric triplet model.

Section 02

Capabilities of IBM Network Intelligence

Network Intelligence bundles an AI-native network operations platform with distinct features for observation, investigation, knowledge management, and integration. Each capability is described both technically and in plain language, with a real-world example.

01

Dashboard & Observation Monitoring

Centralized visibility · Timeframe filters · Metric & Alarm views

A centralized hub providing real-time information on network observations across all devices and objects. Observations are split into Metrics, Alarms, and Total columns. Supports time-period filtering to focus on recent activity or investigate historical patterns. Flags critical anomalies for immediate attention.

Plain language: A single control room screen showing every anomaly happening across your entire network right now — colour-coded by severity, filterable by time, and free of the noise that buries critical issues in traditional monitoring tools.
02

Anomaly Detection with Time-Series AI

Quantile · Prophet · Time-series foundation models · >1M indicators/5min

Continuously monitors metric data streams using Quantile, Prophet, and domain-trained time-series foundation models. Performs analysis in both time and frequency domains, identifying long- and short-term patterns to detect anomalies before they cause user impact. A single distributed node processes over one million indicators every five minutes.

Plain language: Instead of waiting for a threshold alarm (e.g., "CPU > 90%"), Network Intelligence detects subtle, emerging patterns — like a gradual degradation that traditional tools miss entirely — hours before an outage occurs.
03

Investigations & Root-Cause Hypothesis

NOC AI agents · Semantic analysis · ITSM ticket creation

Network Intelligence flags critical observations, auto-generates ITSM tickets with a root-cause hypothesis and remediation plan, and investigates each anomaly to identify what caused it. Each NOC agent is a multi-agent system comprising a reasoning agent, a RAG agent, and a function-calling agent working in concert.

Plain language: When an issue is detected, Network Intelligence doesn't just alert you — it opens a ticket with a draft explanation of why the issue occurred and what steps to take to fix it. The on-call engineer arrives to a pre-investigated incident.
04

Devices Management & Insights

IP routers · RAN sites · Device types · Object-level analysis

Provides a per-device view of all observations — including metrics, alarms, and correlated anomalies — at the object level. Supports different device types (routers, nodes) and allows drilling into specific devices to see the full anomaly history and current health state.

Plain language: Think of this as opening the hood of a specific router or RAN base station and seeing a complete medical history — what went wrong before, what's happening now, and which components are currently showing warning signs.
05

Skills — Repeatable Workflow Automation

Predefined workflow templates · Chat integration · NOC playbooks

Skills are predefined, reusable workflows for common NOC tasks — created once and applied repeatedly through the built-in chat assistant. Engineers encode their expert runbooks as Skills, making institutional knowledge available to the AI agents and the entire team, reducing dependency on individual expertise.

Plain language: Instead of every engineer re-inventing the same 10-step investigation process each time a specific type of alarm fires, a Skill encodes that process once. The AI applies it automatically every time.
06

Grounding Documents (RAG Knowledge Base)

RAG · Vector embeddings · MOPs · Config guides · Knowledge base

Upload Methods of Procedure (MOPs), configuration guides, vendor documentation, and internal knowledge base articles. Network Intelligence chunks, embeds, and stores these documents in a vector database. The RAG agent retrieves the most relevant sections when generating root-cause hypotheses and remediation plans, ensuring responses are grounded in your network's specific context.

Plain language: Every MOP and runbook your team has ever written becomes instantly searchable and automatically cited by the AI when it generates a fix recommendation — no more digging through SharePoint to find the right document under pressure.
07

Chat Assistant with AI Reasoning

Chain-of-thought · Quick / Standard / Extended reasoning · Natural language

An embedded AI-powered chat assistant for natural language queries about network state, anomalies, and investigations. Features a "View reasoning" mode to inspect the chain-of-thought behind every AI response. Configurable reasoning depth (Quick, Standard, Extended) allows operators to balance response speed against analytical depth.

Plain language: Ask "Why is Site 42 degraded?" in plain English and get a structured AI answer — not a raw metric dump. Click "View reasoning" to see exactly how the AI reached its conclusion, ensuring operator trust and accountability.
08

Tool Management & API Integration

OpenAPI spec · ITSM · Inventory systems · ServiceNow · Data lakes

Integrates with external systems using OpenAPI specification, enabling Network Intelligence to pull and push data to ITSM tools (ServiceNow), inventory systems, enterprise information systems (EIS), and data lakes. Automated ticket creation sends the root-cause hypothesis and remediation plan directly into the team's existing workflow.

Plain language: Network Intelligence connects to ServiceNow, your existing ticketing system, and your network inventory database — so when an issue is found, the ticket is already filed with the diagnosis included, without anyone having to switch tools.
09

MCP Server Integration

Model Context Protocol · External monitoring platforms · Bi-directional data

Supports Model Context Protocol (MCP) server integration to connect with external monitoring or management platforms. Network Intelligence receives and analyzes network data flowing from MCP servers, extending its anomaly detection and agentic AI capabilities to any MCP-compatible data source.

Plain language: Already using a network monitoring platform that supports MCP? Plug it directly into Network Intelligence and the AI gains visibility into that platform's data — without replacing your existing tools.
10

Connectors & External Agent Integration

Agent-to-Agent (A2A) protocol · External agents · Connector framework

Integrates external applications via a Connector framework and supports external AI agents using the Agent-to-Agent (A2A) protocol. This allows Network Intelligence to participate in broader multi-agent AI ecosystems — receiving signals from and delegating actions to external agents as part of automated network operations workflows.

Plain language: Other AI agents in your automation stack can talk directly to Network Intelligence — for example, a capacity planning agent can ask Network Intelligence "is this device about to degrade?" before making a routing decision.
11

Observation Metrics & Automatic Metric Groups

Device · Component · Indicator triplets · Auto-grouping · Metric baselines

Organises all network data into device-component-indicator triplets, capturing health and status at a fine-grained level. Automatically assigns metrics to metric groups based on object type on first ingestion — no manual configuration required. Supports both metric-based and alarm-based observations with separate views for each.

Plain language: Every data point has three labels: the device it came from, the component (e.g., an interface), and what it measures (e.g., packet loss). This structure lets the AI understand relationships between measurements — not just the measurements themselves.
12

Two Operating Modes: Autonomous & Assistant

Autonomous mode · Assistant mode · Human-in-the-loop · Configurable autonomy

Network Intelligence supports two operating modes. In Autonomous mode, AI agents detect anomalies, generate hypotheses, and execute remediation plans without human intervention — ideal for repetitive, well-understood incident types. In Assistant mode, the AI surfaces insights and recommendations but waits for human approval before executing actions, maintaining human oversight over critical changes.

Plain language: For routine issues (e.g., restarting a stuck process), let the AI fix it automatically at 3am without waking anyone. For complex changes to production routing, require a human to approve the plan first.

Section 04

Real-World Use Cases & Problems Solved

IBM Network Intelligence is deployed wherever multi-domain network operations require more than threshold-based alerting. Here are eight concrete scenarios where the AI-native approach makes the critical difference.

Telecom / Carrier Operations

Reducing NOC Alert Fatigue at Scale

A tier-1 carrier's NOC receives over 50,000 alerts per day across their IP and RAN networks. Operators spend the majority of their shift acknowledging low-priority alarms, leaving little time for genuine investigation. Network Intelligence's semantic AI filters out false positives, presenting operators with only the high-confidence anomalies that actually require attention — a fraction of the total event volume.

Outcome: Meaningful reduction in alert noise; NOC operators shift from reactive alarm acknowledgment to proactive network management. MTBI improves as genuine issues are caught earlier.

Enterprise IT Operations

3am Outage — No On-Call Engineer Required

A router in a critical enterprise WAN degrades at 3am due to a recurring interface flap. Historically, the on-call engineer would be paged, spend 20 minutes reading logs, then execute a standard restart procedure from the MOP. With Network Intelligence in Autonomous mode, the anomaly is detected, the MOP is retrieved via RAG, and the remediation is executed automatically — the engineer wakes to a closed ticket.

Outcome: Zero on-call disruption for routine incidents. MTTR reduced from 30+ minutes to under 5 minutes. Repeat incidents of the same type handled without human intervention.

Multi-Vendor Network Environments

Root Cause Tracing Across Vendor Boundaries

An enterprise runs a hybrid network with Cisco routers, Nokia RAN equipment, and a third-party SD-WAN overlay. An application degradation is reported by users — but the root cause spans three different vendor domains. Traditional monitoring tools each see their own piece; Network Intelligence ingests all three data streams, correlates the cross-domain anomalies semantically, and traces the root cause to a BGP route change on the Cisco side affecting Nokia RAN backhaul.

Outcome: Root cause identified in minutes versus hours. No need for a cross-functional war room or manual log correlation across three vendor dashboards.

Radio Access Network (RAN)

Early Warning of RAN Degradation Before User Impact

A mobile operator's RAN cells in a dense urban area begin showing subtle, gradually worsening signal quality metrics — below threshold alarm levels, invisible to traditional monitoring. Network Intelligence's time-series foundation models detect the anomalous pattern six hours before it would have triggered a threshold alarm, giving engineering teams time to dispatch field maintenance before customers experience dropped calls.

Outcome: Silent degradation caught before user impact. Proactive maintenance replaces reactive repair. Customer experience protected; SLA penalties avoided.

Knowledge Management & Staff Turnover

Preserving Expert Knowledge When Senior Engineers Leave

A network operations team loses its most experienced engineer to retirement. That engineer's deep knowledge of specific vendor quirks, proprietary workarounds, and undocumented procedures lives in tribal knowledge. Before leaving, the team uploads all known MOPs, runbooks, and troubleshooting guides as Grounding Documents. New engineers ask the chat assistant in plain language and receive answers grounded in the retiring engineer's documented expertise.

Outcome: Institutional knowledge preserved and made universally accessible. Junior engineers resolve issues that previously required the most senior team member. Onboarding time reduced significantly.

ITSM & ServiceNow Integration

Pre-Filled Incident Tickets with AI Diagnosis

A managed service provider (MSP) is contractually required to meet a 15-minute initial response SLA on all Priority 1 incidents. Previously, engineers would manually create tickets, add initial notes, and attempt to gather diagnostic data — eating into the SLA window. Network Intelligence auto-creates the ServiceNow ticket at the moment of detection, pre-filled with the root-cause hypothesis, affected devices, and a recommended remediation plan.

Outcome: P1 SLA compliance rate improves dramatically. Engineers arrive at a pre-investigated incident rather than a blank ticket. Customer reporting quality improved.

AI Safety & Compliance

Explainable AI for Regulated Industries

A financial services firm deploys network operations AI but must meet internal auditability requirements: every automated action must be traceable to a documented decision chain. Network Intelligence's "View reasoning" feature exposes the full chain-of-thought behind every AI recommendation. Built-in prompt guardrails and AI scaffolding ensure the system stays focused on network outcomes — not free-form generation that could produce unreliable results.

Outcome: AI automation adopted in a regulated environment without compliance concerns. Audit trail maintained for every agent action. Trust in AI recommendations built through transparency.

Cloud & Hybrid Infrastructure

Unified Observability Across On-Prem and Cloud

A global enterprise runs critical workloads on a combination of on-premises IP infrastructure, private cloud, and AWS. Each layer has its own monitoring stack. Network Intelligence ingests data from all three via its flexible data pipeline — regardless of format or cleanliness — and applies unified anomaly detection across the hybrid environment, correlating on-prem router issues with cloud networking events in a single investigation view.

Outcome: Single pane of glass for hybrid network intelligence. Cross-domain incidents no longer require multiple teams working in isolation. Infrastructure complexity becomes tractable for a smaller operations team.
