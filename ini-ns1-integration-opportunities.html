IBM Network Intelligence × NS1 Connect

Integration
Opportunities

A structured analysis of how IBM Network Intelligence and IBM NS1 Connect can be integrated to create outcomes neither product achieves alone — spanning traffic protection, intelligence sharing, agentic automation, unified observability, and guided AI adoption — mapped to the five NS1 personas and the four phases of AI maturity.

Integration Use Cases · 18 Scenarios · 5 Personas

Overview

Two products.
One closed loop.

INI knows what is wrong and why at the network layer. NS1 controls where traffic flows at the DNS layer. The gap between those capabilities — detect a degradation, then act on it via DNS — is what every use case in this document bridges.

IBM Network Intelligence (INI)
An AI-native SaaS platform that transforms multi-vendor network telemetry into anomaly detections, root-cause hypotheses, and agentic remediation. Core strength: understanding what is wrong and why — across IP and RAN domains — using time-series foundation models, NOC AI agents, and a RAG knowledge base.
IBM NS1 Connect
An authoritative DNS and traffic steering platform that controls where traffic flows — routing users to the fastest, most available, and most cost-effective endpoint at the moment of each DNS query. Core strength: programmable, real-time traffic management via Filter Chains, health monitors, and Pulsar RUM data.

Four technical integration pathways

01
OpenAPI / REST
Both products expose fully documented REST APIs. INI can call NS1's record and monitor APIs; NS1 can receive INI anomaly signals via webhook or polling.
02
MCP Server Protocol
INI natively supports MCP server integration. NS1 query telemetry, routing decisions, and anomaly policies can be fed into INI as an MCP-connected data source — and NS1 / INI capabilities can be exposed as scoped MCP servers for each team's approved AI tool (IBM Bob, Claude Code, Codex, Copilot).
03
Agent-to-Agent (A2A)
INI's agentic framework supports A2A protocol. An NS1 automation agent can act as an external agent in INI's multi-agent remediation workflow.
04
ITSM Ticket + Skills
INI auto-creates ITSM tickets with remediation plans. NS1 DNS change steps can be embedded as Skills in INI's knowledge base, included in every generated ticket.

Who this is for

Five NS1 personas. One shared requirement: trust.

Every use case in this document is mapped to the NS1 personas below. Across all five, the same trust factors recur — audit trails, scoped permissions, verifiable recommendations, and human approval before actions. Use cases are designed so that AI explains and recommends first, and only acts within boundaries the personas have explicitly approved.

Engineering
DNS SRE

Most hands-on role. Configures records, monitors health, investigates incidents, and owns day-to-day functioning of DNS.

Needs from AI
  • Faster, context-aware troubleshooting
  • Small, reviewable outputs
  • Must understand output before approving
Architecture
DNS Architect

Owns full technical design of DNS infrastructure and traffic strategy. Final escalation point for complex issues.

Needs from AI
  • Recommendations backed by evidence
  • MCP servers for tool of choice
  • Faster workflow experimentation
Operations
DNS SecOps

Scoped to security, compliance, and risk. Defines and enforces the boundaries within which DNS can be safely changed or automated.

Needs from AI
  • Traceable, auditable AI actions
  • Human approval for sensitive actions
  • Security implications of DNS changes
Architecture
AI Architect

Horizontal scope. Designs how AI, MCP integrations, and agent workflows plug into engineering and operations. Cannot decide DNS architecture.

Needs from AI
  • Secure MCP integrations
  • Trusted autonomous workflows
  • Reduced manual operational work
Operations
Executive

Business level. Evaluates vendors, justifies spend, and decides whether AI creates enough business value. Rarely in day-to-day operations.

Needs from AI
  • Clear confidence levels and limitations
  • Insights with "next steps"
  • Onboarding and workload reduction

Relationship to risk

SRE and Architect are personally accountable for outages and are the most risk-averse. SecOps manages risk institutionally through policy; the AI Architect focuses on governance and trust failures; the Executive on business and reputational risk.

DNS Architect = DNS SRE > DNS SecOps > AI Architect > Executive

Technical depth

Architect and SRE have the deepest DNS knowledge. The AI Architect is deep in LLMs, agents, and MCP but not DNS. SecOps' lens is governance, not configuration. The Executive's lens is vendor evaluation and ROI.

DNS Architect = DNS SRE > AI Architect > DNS SecOps > Executive

AI maturity phases & control levels

Phase 1 · Explain
Experiment & prepare
AI answers product and domain questions instead of manual documentation search. Roles: DNS SRE, DNS Architect.
Phase 2 · Explain
Build pilots & capabilities
AI explains what existing configurations, filter chains, and steering setups do. Roles: SRE, Architect, SecOps, AI Architect.
Phase 3 · Investigate
Develop AI ways of working
AI scans zones and analyses Insights data with read-only access, surfacing anomalies and next steps. Roles: SRE, Architect, SecOps, AI Architect.
Phase 4 · Act / Verify
Become AI future-ready
AI simulates, validates, and generates changes — executed only after human approval or within SecOps-approved policy. Roles: SRE, Architect, SecOps, AI Architect.

The Executive evaluates across all phases to track progression of AI maturity and business value.

Control levels used in each use case: Explain AI describes, no access to change · Recommend read-only analysis with proposed actions · Approve AI prepares a change, a named persona approves before execution · Policy-bound AI executes only pre-approved change types within SecOps-defined scope, with full audit trail and rollback.

INI × NS1 Connect — Integration Opportunities 2

Category 01

Traffic
Protection

INI detects degradation patterns early — often before a server fails a health check. These use cases exploit that early-warning capability to steer DNS traffic away from at-risk infrastructure proactively.

01 Predictive Failover
AI-Predicted Pre-emptive DNS Failover
DNS SRE DNS SecOps Phase 4 · Act / Verify Approve → Policy-bound
NS1's health monitors detect endpoint failures reactively — after an endpoint stops responding. INI's time-series models identify subtle degradation patterns (rising latency, packet loss trends, CPU saturation gradients) minutes to hours before a service fails its health check. Integrating the two creates a pre-emptive failover loop: INI fires a warning when a server is on a degradation trajectory; NS1 adjusts the Filter Chain before it fails.
INI
Time-series anomaly detection identifies degrading endpoints before failure. NOC AI agent generates a root-cause hypothesis with a stated confidence level and proposes a recommended DNS action, with supporting evidence, for DNS SRE review.
NS1
On approval (or within a SecOps-approved policy for this incident type), the Filter Chain is updated via a scoped API key to set the degrading endpoint's weight to zero or assign a lower priority tier. Traffic shifts to healthy alternatives before the endpoint fails its health check.

Guardrails: the SRE approves until SecOps has defined the endpoints and weight changes that can run automatically; every change is logged with the reasoning behind it and can be rolled back.

Mechanism INI OpenAPI → NS1 REST INI NOC Agent → A2A → NS1 DNS Agent INI Skill: adjust-filter-chain-weight

Outcome: Customer-impacting failover events are reduced. Traffic exits before the server collapses, eliminating the window where users hit a failing endpoint waiting for DNS TTL to expire.

02 DDoS Correlation
Network-Layer DDoS Signal → DNS Traffic Diversion
DNS SecOps DNS SRE Phase 4 · Act / Verify Approve
INI monitors the underlying network — detecting volumetric anomalies, sudden traffic spikes, and infrastructure saturation that precede or accompany an attack. By sharing attack signals, INI sees the network being hit while NS1 simultaneously changes DNS answers to divert users to unaffected regions or a scrubbing centre endpoint.
INI
Anomaly detection flags abnormal traffic volume across IP domain routers. Root-cause hypothesis identifies attack type and affected PoP/region. Alert triggers API call to NS1 with affected region identifier.
NS1
Geographic filter in the Filter Chain overrides routing for the affected region — diverting queries to clean-pipe endpoints or scrubbing infrastructure. The proposed diversion highlights its security implications for SecOps before it is applied.

Guardrails: SecOps pre-defines approved diversion targets (scrubbing centres, clean-pipe PoPs); the AI can only select from that list. Every diversion is recorded in an audit trail along with the source signal data.

Mechanism INI alert webhook → NS1 API geo-filter update INI Dashboard + NS1 Insights joint monitoring

Outcome: Unified attack response across network and DNS layers. Detection-to-diversion time shrinks because SecOps approves a pre-built, pre-validated diversion instead of building one manually during the attack.

03 RAN Degradation
RAN Site Degradation Triggering CDN Re-steering
DNS Architect DNS SRE Phase 4 · Act / Verify Approve
When INI detects a RAN site degradation — such as a base station with rising packet loss or a backhaul congestion event — NS1 can redirect content delivery for that geographic region to a CDN PoP with better connectivity, improving end-user quality of experience during network degradation events.
INI
RAN domain anomaly detection identifies degraded base station or backhaul segment. Device-level analysis provides geographic identifier (site ID, lat/lon, region code) of the affected area.
NS1
Subnet or geographic Filter Chain rule updated to route mobile subscribers in the affected area to an alternative CDN PoP with better backhaul connectivity.
Mechanism INI RAN anomaly → region code → NS1 geo-filter API INI Grounding Doc: RAN-region-to-CDN mapping

Guardrails: the DNS Architect owns and approves the RAN-region-to-CDN mapping. The SRE approves each re-steer until a region's pattern has been proven safe.

Outcome: Content delivery QoE is preserved during RAN degradation events. Mobile users in an affected cell area are routed to a better-performing CDN using a mapping the Architect has approved.

Category 02

Intelligence
Sharing

INI and NS1 each collect a distinct signal stream. These use cases connect those streams — giving INI richer context for root-cause analysis and giving NS1 deeper network telemetry to drive better routing decisions.

04 DNS Telemetry into INI
NS1 DNS Query Telemetry as an INI Data Source
DNS SRE AI Architect DNS SecOps Phase 3 · Investigate Recommend
NS1 Insights produces detailed DNS query analytics — query volumes by region, resolution latencies, routing decisions, and anomaly flags. Feeding NS1 telemetry into INI as an MCP-connected data source gives INI's AI models a complete picture: both the network-side anomaly and the application-side DNS impact, enabling richer root-cause correlations and faster MTTR.
INI
Ingests NS1 DNS telemetry as a new data stream via MCP Server integration. Correlates DNS resolution anomalies with network-layer events (e.g., CNAME resolution failures align with router interface drops on the same AS path).
NS1
Streams DNS Insights analytics — query volume changes, routing decision logs, health monitor state changes — as time-series metrics to INI's data pipeline via MCP or REST API. Access is read-only and granted by SecOps, which also reviews how customer data is handled.
Mechanism NS1 Insights API → INI MCP Server INI device triplet: NS1-zone / record / query-volume

Outcome: INI root-cause hypotheses include DNS-layer evidence. Engineers receive tickets with complete end-to-end causality: network fault → DNS consequence → user impact — in a single ticket.

05 Pulsar + INI Latency Fusion
Fusing Pulsar RUM Data with INI Network Latency Models
DNS SRE DNS Architect Phase 3 · Investigate Recommend → Approve
NS1 Pulsar collects real-user latency measurements from browsers — the actual application-layer experience. INI monitors network-layer latency across routers and links. Correlating both datasets makes it possible to distinguish between "network is slow" and "CDN is slow" — a distinction that determines whether the right remediation is a network change (INI) or a DNS re-steer (NS1).
INI
Provides network-layer latency telemetry from routers, switches, and WAN links. INI's AI determines whether observed application latency is attributable to network infrastructure or to the CDN/application tier.
NS1
Pulsar RUM data provides real-user CDN/endpoint latency per region. When the CDN is the driver, NS1's Filter Chain is updated. When the network is the driver, NS1 defers to INI remediation.
Mechanism Pulsar latency API → INI data pipeline INI reasoning agent: CDN vs. network attribution Conditional NS1 Filter Chain update via A2A

Outcome: Smarter remediation routing. The AI recommends whether to fix the DNS (NS1) or the network (INI), showing the evidence and a confidence level. This reduces false remediations where DNS is re-steered but the root cause is actually a router in the path.

06 Topology Context
Network Topology Context Enriching NS1 Routing Decisions
DNS Architect DNS SRE Phase 4 · Act / Verify Approve
NS1's routing decisions are based on DNS-layer signals — health checks, latency probes, RUM data. They lack awareness of the underlying network topology: BGP AS paths, router utilisation, link capacity, and physical site interconnections. Exposing INI's semantic network context to NS1's Filter Chain engine means DNS routing can factor in network-layer capacity and congestion — not just endpoint availability.
INI
Semantic network context provides real-time topology data: which links are congested, which routers are approaching capacity, which AS paths have higher-than-normal latency. Exported via API as structured metadata tags per endpoint or region.
NS1
Record metadata fields and custom Filter Chain logic consume INI-provided network health tags — steering traffic away from endpoints that are "up" but reachable via a congested network path.
Mechanism INI topology API → NS1 record metadata update NS1 custom filter: network-congestion-score tag

Guardrails: the DNS Architect designs and approves the custom filter logic that consumes INI tags. Tag updates are logged so routing decisions can be traced back to the network evidence behind them.

Outcome: DNS routing that reflects the actual network, not just surface-level endpoint health. Users are steered to endpoints with genuinely clean network paths — combining the best of application-layer and network-layer observability.

Category 03

Agentic
Automation

INI's NOC AI agents can detect, investigate, and propose remediation. NS1's API enables programmatic DNS change. These use cases describe closed-loop scenarios governed by DNS SecOps policy: a human approves by default, and automatic execution is limited to pre-approved, scoped, audited change types.

07 Policy-Bound Autonomous Mode
INI NOC Agent Updates NS1 Filter Chains Within Approved Policy
AI Architect DNS SecOps DNS SRE Phase 4 · Act / Verify Policy-bound
Some network incidents are well understood and repeat often, and their remediation plans have already been validated. For these, INI can run in Autonomous mode, but only for incident types DNS SecOps has explicitly pre-approved. The AI Architect designs the workflow and SecOps defines its scope. The DNS SRE stays accountable and keeps the right to approve or refuse adding each incident type. An INI Skill encodes the NS1 API call sequence as a reusable, reviewable workflow step.
INI
The NOC agent detects the anomaly, generates a hypothesis, and checks that the incident type is on the approved list. It runs a dry-run of the "DNS-failover" Skill, then executes it through a scoped NS1 API key. The action, its reasoning, and a rollback plan are logged to the ITSM ticket. Any incident outside the policy falls back to Assistant mode (UC08).
NS1
REST API accepts programmatic record and Filter Chain updates from the INI agent. The change propagates globally in under 20 seconds. NS1 Insights confirms traffic re-routing success and feeds confirmation back to INI.
Mechanism INI Skill: ns1-update-filter-chain INI Autonomous mode → NS1 REST API NS1 confirmation → INI ITSM ticket close

Guardrails: scoped API permissions (specific zones, records, and change types only), limits on how often and how much can change, a mandatory audit trail, and automatic rollback if NS1 Insights does not confirm recovery. SecOps reviews the policy on a regular schedule.

Outcome: Hands-off DNS failover for the incident types SecOps has approved, within boundaries they control. The SRE reviews a ticket showing anomaly detected → policy check passed → DNS updated → traffic rerouted → recovery confirmed, along with the full reasoning and a rollback option.

08 Assistant Mode
Human-in-the-Loop DNS Change Approval Workflow
DNS SRE DNS SecOps Phase 4 · Act / Verify Approve
For complex, high-risk, or novel incidents, INI's Assistant mode presents the full remediation plan, including the proposed NS1 DNS changes, for human review before anything runs. The DNS SRE reviews the AI's root-cause analysis, the specific NS1 API calls, and a plain-language explanation of their effect. The SRE approves in the ITSM ticket and the INI agent executes the change. Changes that SecOps flags as sensitive also need SecOps approval.
INI
Assistant mode generates an ITSM ticket with root-cause hypothesis, reasoning chain, and a pre-flight validated NS1 API payload. Human approves or edits. On approval, INI agent executes the NS1 call and logs the outcome.
NS1
Receives the validated API call from INI once a human approves. Because the payload was validated against the NS1 schema and dry-run beforehand, manual configuration errors such as typos and missing fields are greatly reduced.
Mechanism INI ITSM ticket + NS1 payload preview Human approval → INI agent → NS1 REST API INI "View reasoning" for audit trail

Outcome: Faster DNS changes for complex incidents with the same human accountability. The SRE approves a pre-validated, explained, AI-drafted change instead of writing it from scratch under pressure. The speed-up should be measured in a pilot, not assumed.

09 Capacity Management
Predictive DNS Load Balancing Based on INI Capacity Forecasts
DNS Architect DNS SRE Phase 4 · Act / Verify Policy-bound
INI's time-series models detect capacity trends — gradual saturation of routers, rising utilisation on key infrastructure links, or seasonal traffic patterns predicting demand spikes. These forecasts can proactively adjust NS1's weighted load balancing before saturation occurs, redistributing DNS traffic away from infrastructure trending toward its capacity limit.
INI
Long-term time-series analysis forecasts which data centre or network region is trending toward saturation over the next 1–12 hours. Publishes a capacity score per region via API — refreshed every N minutes.
NS1
The Weighted Shuffle filter in the Filter Chain uses INI's capacity scores as weights, shifting traffic proportions across regions as the forecast changes. Shifts stay within the weight ranges the Architect has approved.

Guardrails: the DNS Architect sets the minimum and maximum weight per region and the largest shift allowed per interval. Any change outside those limits is sent to the SRE for approval, and every weight change is logged.

Mechanism INI capacity forecast API → NS1 weighted filter update Scheduled INI agent: capacity-to-weight translation

Outcome: Proactive load balancing that heads off network saturation before it becomes an outage. Traffic is redistributed gradually within limits the Architect has approved, instead of in a sudden failover spike when a threshold is breached.

10 Unified Incident Context
Single ITSM Ticket with Network + DNS Evidence
DNS SRE DNS Architect Phase 3 · Investigate Recommend
Today, a production incident typically generates separate tickets or alerts in network monitoring tools and DNS management tools — engineers must manually correlate them. By integrating INI's ITSM ticket generation with NS1's Insights data, a single auto-generated ticket contains both the network-layer root cause and the DNS-layer impact: which endpoints were removed, how traffic shifted, and the user-facing consequence.
INI
Generates ITSM ticket with network root-cause hypothesis, affected devices, and anomaly timeline. NOC agent queries NS1 Insights API to append DNS impact data to the same ticket before filing it.
NS1
NS1 Insights API provides: which endpoints failed health checks, how Filter Chain routing changed, query volume impact by region, and whether failover was triggered.
Mechanism INI RAG: NS1 Insights API as grounding context INI ITSM enrichment: DNS impact section in ticket body

Outcome: Incident tickets are complete on arrival. The on-call DNS SRE sees the full story (network fault, DNS consequence, traffic impact) in one place, which gives context-aware troubleshooting. Complex cases escalate to the DNS Architect with all the evidence attached, and post-incident reviews are faster and more accurate.

11 Change Risk Detection
INI Monitors Network Impact of NS1 DNS Changes
DNS SRE DNS SecOps Phase 3 · Investigate Recommend
NS1 DNS changes — especially large-scale GSLB weight updates, geo-routing policy changes, or major failover events — can cause significant traffic shifts that affect downstream network infrastructure. By tagging planned NS1 DNS changes as events in INI's timeline, INI's anomaly detection can distinguish between anomalies caused by the DNS change and independent network faults, reducing false-positive alerts during change windows.
INI
Ingests NS1 change events as markers in the INI observation timeline. During and after a DNS change window, anomaly detection is calibrated to suppress expected traffic shifts while still alerting on genuine faults unrelated to the change.
NS1
Publishes DNS change events (record updates, Filter Chain modifications, health monitor state changes) via webhook or API — providing INI with the timing and scope of each change for contextual anomaly suppression.
Mechanism NS1 change webhook → INI event timeline INI contextual anomaly suppression during change windows

Outcome: Fewer false-positive alerts during DNS changes. The DNS SRE isn't flooded with traffic-shift alerts each time a planned change redistributes load, and only genuine faults surface. SecOps gets a traceable record linking every DNS change to its observed network impact.

12 Cross-domain Chat Assistant
Natural Language DNS + Network Queries via INI Chat
DNS SRE DNS Architect Executive Phase 1–3 · Explain / Investigate Explain
By connecting NS1's data as a grounding source and tool in the INI chat, SREs and Architects can ask cross-domain questions like "Why are users in Europe experiencing high latency?" and receive a synthesised answer spanning both the network layer (INI: router interface congestion on EU peering links) and the DNS layer (NS1: Pulsar data shows UK users being routed to US CDN endpoints due to a stale geo-filter).
INI
Chat assistant with chain-of-thought reasoning queries both INI's own anomaly data and NS1's Insights API as a registered tool. Synthesises a unified answer covering network and DNS layers. "View reasoning" shows which data sources were consulted.
NS1
NS1 REST API and Insights data are registered as Tools in INI's function-calling agent. INI chat can call NS1 in real time — retrieving current routing decisions, health monitor states, and query analytics on demand.
Mechanism NS1 API registered as INI Tool Management integration INI RAG: NS1 docs as grounding document INI Chat: cross-domain natural language queries

Outcome: A single pane of glass for network and DNS questions. SREs and Architects no longer switch between INI's dashboard and NS1's portal, because the AI assembles the answer from both systems and cites its sources. Executives get plain-language insights that explain what happened and the recommended next steps.

Category 05

Guided AI
Adoption

Trust is built step by step. These use cases follow the AI maturity path: AI first explains, then investigates with read-only access, and only then simulates and generates changes. Each step gives SREs, Architects, and SecOps the evidence they need before granting more autonomy.

13 Product Knowledge
NS1 Knowledge Assistant Grounded in INI's RAG
DNS SRE DNS Architect Phase 1 · Explain Explain
SREs new to NS1 lose time searching long documentation sets for terminology, feature behaviour, and how-to guidance. Loading NS1 documentation into INI's RAG knowledge base lets the chat answer NS1 product and DNS-concept questions with citations to the source pages, so users can check every answer.
INI
RAG knowledge base indexes NS1 docs, API references, and internal runbooks. Chat answers cite the documents they used and state when the answer is uncertain or outside the indexed material.
NS1
Supplies versioned product documentation, filter-chain reference material, and glossary content as grounding documents. No account access is required.
Mechanism INI RAG: NS1 docs as grounding documents Cited answers in INI Chat

Outcome: Faster onboarding and less time spent reading documentation. It is a low-risk first step with no access to infrastructure, and it shows Executives an early, measurable reduction in onboarding and support workload.

14 Configuration Explainer
Explain Existing Filter Chains & Steering in Plain Language
DNS SRE DNS Architect DNS SecOps Phase 2 · Explain Explain
Filter chains, answer groups, and metadata are hard to read, and inherited setups are often poorly documented. With read-only access to the current configuration, the AI explains what a record or filter chain does, which answers a user in a given region will get, and why. That helps Architects explain design decisions across teams, and helps SecOps see what a configuration exposes.
INI
Chat retrieves the configuration through a read-only NS1 tool and produces a plain-language explanation, a per-region answer walkthrough, and links to the exact fields it relied on so the SRE can check it.
NS1
A read-only API key exposes zones, records, filter chains, answer metadata, and monitor configuration. No write scopes are granted.
Mechanism NS1 read-only API → INI Tool INI Chat: config-to-plain-language

Outcome: Every team shares a single, checkable explanation of how traffic is steered. SREs build confidence that the AI understands their configurations before giving it any write access.

15 Zone Hygiene
AI Zone & Configuration Risk Scan
DNS SecOps DNS SRE DNS Architect Phase 3 · Investigate Recommend
Lame delegations, orphaned records, dangling CNAMEs, and inconsistent filter chains build up over time and create both security exposure and inefficiency. SecOps defines what the AI is allowed to scan. The AI then reviews zones and configurations, combines the findings with INI's network view (for example, records that point to decommissioned infrastructure), and produces a prioritised report with suggested fixes.
INI
A scheduled agent cross-references NS1 records with INI's inventory and topology to flag records that point to devices which are unreachable, decommissioned, or degraded. Findings are filed as ITSM tickets ranked by risk.
NS1
Read-only zone and record export within the scope SecOps has defined. Remediation stays a recommendation: SREs decide whether to act, and any change goes through UC08 or UC16.
Mechanism NS1 zone export → INI scan agent INI inventory cross-reference Risk-ranked ITSM findings

Outcome: Ongoing DNS hygiene and security posture checks with no write access. SecOps gets reproducible, auditable scan results, and SREs get a prioritised list of improvements they can act on.

16 Simulate & Verify
Dry-run Simulation of DNS Changes Before Deployment
DNS SRE DNS Architect DNS SecOps Phase 4 · Act / Verify Recommend → Approve
Production DNS cannot tolerate high-impact mistakes. Before any change is applied, whether AI-drafted or written by a person, it is simulated. The simulation shows which answers each region and subnet will receive, compares expected and predicted traffic distribution, and uses INI's network view to show whether the new targets can handle the shifted load. Conflicts and unintended routing behaviour are flagged before deployment.
INI
Estimates the network impact of the predicted traffic shift (link utilisation and capacity headroom at the target endpoints) and attaches a before/after comparison to the change ticket.
NS1
Evaluates the proposed filter chain or record change against representative queries and returns the predicted answers per region, without changing anything live.
Mechanism INI Skill: ns1-dry-run Expected vs. predicted diff in ITSM ticket Gate for UC07 / UC08 / UC17

Outcome: Every change is verified before it reaches production. The SRE approves a change they can see and understand, and SecOps gets reproducible evidence that the change behaves as intended.

17 Intent-based Configuration
Generate Filter Chains from Natural-Language Business Requirements
DNS Architect DNS SRE Executive Phase 4 · Act / Verify Approve
An Architect or business stakeholder describes an intent in plain language, for example: "Keep EU users in EU data centres, prefer the cheapest healthy CDN, and fail over to US-East only if both EU sites are degraded." The AI drafts the matching filter chain, answer metadata, and monitors, explains each step, and runs it through the UC16 dry-run. Nothing is deployed until the Architect or SRE approves the change.
INI
The agent turns the stated intent into a draft configuration, using INI's topology and capacity data to check that the proposed targets are realistic, and records how the intent maps to the configuration in the ticket.
NS1
Receives the generated filter chain only after dry-run and human approval. The configuration can be versioned, so it is easy to compare against the previous state and roll back.
Mechanism INI Chat: intent → NS1 config draft UC16 dry-run gate Approval → NS1 REST API

Outcome: Teams can use advanced DNS capabilities with less specialist expertise, and business requirements go into effect faster. Architects stay in control of design decisions, and the AI handles the translation.

18 Bring Your Own AI Tool
Scoped NS1 + INI MCP Servers for Approved AI Tools
AI Architect DNS Architect DNS SecOps DNS SRE Phase 2–4 · All Explain → Approve
Architects and SREs already use company-approved AI tools such as IBM Bob, Claude Code, Codex, and Copilot. Publishing NS1 and INI capabilities as MCP servers lets those tools query DNS configuration, Insights, and network anomalies, and draft changes in the user's own workflow. Read-only tools are available by default. Write tools are off unless SecOps enables them, and they always go through the dry-run and approval gates.
INI
Exposes anomaly, topology, and ticket tools through MCP. It also provides the shared audit log, so every call from any AI tool is attributed to a user and can be traced.
NS1
Provides an MCP server for zones, records, filter chains, Insights, and dry-run. Each tool's scope maps to an NS1 API key permission that SecOps manages.
Mechanism NS1 MCP server (scoped) INI MCP server Shared audit trail

Outcome: Secure MCP integrations that work with each team's tool of choice. The AI Architect can design agent workflows quickly, while SecOps keeps central control of permissions and auditing.

Reduce support cost

Reduce operational risk
UC01, 02, 11, 15, and 16 catch degradations, attacks, and risky configurations earlier, with an audit trail SecOps can review.
Accelerate time-to-value
UC17 and 18 let teams use advanced DNS capabilities with less specialist expertise, through their approved AI tools.
Differentiate the vendor
Closed-loop network-to-DNS intelligence that neither product offers alone. Its value can be measured at each phase rather than taken from marketing claims.
